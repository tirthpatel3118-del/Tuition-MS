## 1. Parties and scope

This Data Processing Agreement (DPA) is intended to apply between:
- **Institute / Customer:** the legal organisation identified in its ClassBandhu workspace or approved order/acceptance record (Institute); and
- **Service Provider:** Tirth Patel, operating ClassBandhu Tuition as an individual for this draft (Provider).

The Provider's full legal/service address and the Institute's legal name and address must be completed or reliably captured before execution. This DPA applies to ClassBandhu Tuition only, not a separate ClassBandhu School product.

This DPA supplements the ClassBandhu Tuition Terms & Conditions. It applies to personal data processed by the Provider on the Institute's documented instructions to provide the subscribed Platform (Customer Personal Data). It does not automatically govern information for which the Provider independently determines purposes, such as account administration, platform subscription billing, website access requests, security and direct support records. Those activities are addressed in the Privacy Policy and the parties' other arrangements.

## 2. Roles of the parties

2.1 For Institute records used for the Institute's educational and administrative purposes, the Institute is expected to determine the purposes and essential means of processing and to act as the responsible data fiduciary/controller where applicable law uses those concepts.

2.2 The Provider is expected to act as a data processor/service provider to the extent it processes Customer Personal Data on the Institute's documented instructions solely to provide, secure, support and maintain the Platform.

2.3 The Provider may act independently for Platform Account Data, platform billing, invitation administration, public website enquiries, security and support where it determines the purpose and means of those activities.

2.4 The parties must not rely only on contractual labels. If the Provider determines an independent purpose for any processing of Customer Personal Data, the parties must document that purpose and the Provider's role and legal basis separately.

2.5 The DPDP Act, 2023 and Rules, 2025 have staggered commencement dates. This DPA is drafted to support the intended processing arrangement and future application of those instruments as their substantive provisions commence; counsel must confirm the legal terminology, obligations and applicable law on the effective date. It must also be checked against laws that remain applicable during the transition.

## 3. Processing details

The processing particulars are in Annex 1 and form part of this DPA. They include the subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of processing, data subjects, data categories and frequency.

The Provider may process Customer Personal Data only to the extent necessary for the services enabled for the Institute and the instructions provided by its authorised users and configuration of the Platform.

## 4. Documented instructions and Institute responsibilities

4.1 The Provider will process Customer Personal Data only on documented instructions from the Institute, including instructions arising from actions taken by authorised users and the Institute's Platform configuration, unless applicable law requires otherwise. Where legally permitted, the Provider will inform the Institute of a legal requirement that requires processing.

4.2 The Institute is responsible for:
- identifying and documenting lawful purposes and applicable legal bases;
- providing required notices to students, parents/guardians, staff and other individuals;
- obtaining and retaining any consent, permission or authorisation required, including verifiable parent/lawful-guardian consent where applicable;
- ensuring its instructions are lawful and that it has authority to provide the data to the Provider;
- ensuring data is accurate, relevant and not excessive;
- authorising users and assigning appropriate roles;
- responding to requests from individuals where the Institute is responsible for the relevant processing;
- determining appropriate retention periods for Institute records, subject to law and the Platform's capabilities; and
- maintaining its own evidence of notices, permissions and consents where required.

4.3 The Institute Owner's onboarding declaration is a contractual attestation only. It does not constitute the consent of a parent, guardian or student and does not mean the Provider has verified every consent record.

## 5. Confidentiality and authorised personnel

5.1 The Provider will ensure that persons authorised to access Customer Personal Data are subject to appropriate confidentiality obligations.

5.2 Provider access to Customer Personal Data will be limited to what is reasonably necessary for service delivery, support, security, maintenance and legal compliance. Exceptional support access must be authorised, limited to the need and handled under the Provider's security procedures.

5.3 The Provider will not sell Customer Personal Data or use it for unrelated advertising. Any new analytics, profiling, AI or product-improvement use involving Customer Personal Data must be assessed, disclosed and supported by an appropriate instruction or legal basis before implementation.

## 6. Technical and organisational security measures

6.1 The Provider will maintain technical and organisational measures appropriate to the nature of Customer Personal Data and the risks of the processing, subject to the security schedule in Annex 2 and verification of actual controls.

6.2 The currently identified Platform architecture includes Supabase Auth, managed PostgreSQL, row-level security, role checks, database functions/RPCs and a web application hosted/deployed through Netlify. These architectural facts do not by themselves certify that every control is correctly configured or eliminate the need for continuing testing.

6.3 No representation is made in this draft about a particular encryption algorithm, independent penetration test, security certification, backup frequency, recovery objective, uptime or disaster-recovery capability unless separately verified and documented.

6.4 The Institute will use strong unique passwords, protect credentials and devices, review roles, remove former users promptly and notify the Provider of suspected compromise without undue delay.

## 7. Sub-processors

7.1 The Provider may use sub-processors only where necessary to provide the Platform and subject to appropriate confidentiality, security and data-protection obligations.

7.2 Known services requiring a complete legal and contractual register include:
- **Supabase** — database, authentication and related backend services. Primary and Testing project metadata checked on 27 November 2026 reports the database region as ap-northeast-1 (Tokyo). Exact contracting entity, backups, logs, provider support access, other locations and the current sub-processor list must be verified.
- **Netlify** — web application hosting and deployment. Contracting entity, processing locations, logs, support access, applicable data-processing terms and sub-processors must be verified.
- **Google/Gmail** — the Provider's published contact mailbox is a Gmail address. Confirm whether and how customer support content is processed through that mailbox and include it in the final register if applicable.
- **WhatsApp** — an external communication service opened by the Institute's authorised users for manual messaging. WhatsApp handles information sent through its service under its own terms; the Provider does not control that independent processing.

7.3 Annex 3 must be completed with provider legal names, functions, data categories, locations, applicable contracts and change-notice process before execution. This draft does not constitute unrestricted approval of unidentified sub-processors.

7.4 The parties must agree a reasonable notice and objection process for material sub-processor changes. The notice channel, notice period, objection period and any remedy must be completed in Annex 3 or the main Terms.

## 8. Data-subject requests and assistance

8.1 The Institute is responsible for handling requests concerning Customer Personal Data where it is the responsible data fiduciary/controller.

8.2 Taking account of the nature of processing and the information available to the Provider, the Provider will provide reasonable assistance through available product functions and support processes to help the Institute respond to requests for access, correction, completion, updating, erasure, withdrawal of consent and grievance handling where applicable.

8.3 If the Provider receives a request directly about Customer Personal Data, the Provider may direct the requester to the Institute and notify the Institute without undue delay, unless prohibited by law or the request relates to a purpose for which the Provider is independently responsible.

8.4 The Provider may verify the identity and authority of a requester before disclosing, correcting, exporting or deleting information. The parties should avoid collecting unnecessary identity documents.

## 9. Personal-data incidents and breach cooperation

9.1 The Provider will maintain an incident-response process proportionate to the Platform. The Provider will notify the Institute without undue delay after becoming aware of a confirmed personal-data breach affecting Customer Personal Data, using the contact route in Annex 4.

9.2 The notice will, to the extent known and legally permissible, describe the incident, affected data/categories, likely consequences, containment/remediation measures and a contact for follow-up. Information may be supplied in phases as the investigation progresses.

9.3 The parties will cooperate in investigation, evidence preservation, risk assessment, mitigation and legally required notifications. The Institute remains responsible for notifications it must make as the responsible data fiduciary/controller. The Provider will provide reasonable assistance and information in its possession.

9.4 The Provider will not publicly identify the Institute in connection with an incident without consent unless disclosure is required by law or necessary to protect individuals or the Platform.

9.5 The parties must approve incident contacts, escalation process and operational response targets in Annex 4. This draft does not invent a statutory notification deadline. Each party must meet deadlines applicable to it under laws in force at the relevant time.

## 10. Retention, return and deletion

10.1 The Provider will retain Customer Personal Data for the service term and as otherwise required by documented instructions, applicable law, security needs, dispute resolution or legal hold.

10.2 At the end of the service, the Institute may request return/export or deletion of Customer Personal Data, subject to technical capability, applicable law, legal holds, financial-record duties and the schedule in Annex 5.

10.3 The Provider may retain limited records required for accounting, security, fraud prevention, legal compliance, dispute resolution or legal claims. Such records will be access-restricted and retained only for the purpose that justifies retention.

10.4 Backups and disaster-recovery copies may not be immediately alterable at individual-record level. Backup expiry/deletion periods must be stated only after provider terms and configuration have been verified. Retained backup information must remain protected and must not be restored to active use except for recovery, security or legal purposes.

10.5 Suspension for subscription non-payment does not automatically delete Customer Personal Data. Export and deletion requests remain subject to authority verification, technical capability, applicable law and approved procedures.

## 11. International processing and data location

The configured Supabase database region for the Primary and Testing projects inspected on 27 November 2026 is Tokyo (ap-northeast-1). This is not a complete map of data copies, backups, logs, provider support access or Netlify processing. The parties must review and approve the final data-flow and transfer schedule in Annex 3.

The Provider will disclose known processing locations and material cross-border processing and will follow applicable transfer restrictions and lawful instructions. No India-only hosting promise is made in this draft.

## 12. Audit and compliance information

12.1 On reasonable written request, the Provider will make available information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with this DPA, subject to confidentiality, security and protection of other customers' data.

12.2 Any audit must be reasonable in scope, frequency, notice and cost; avoid access to other Institutes' data; and not compromise Platform security. The parties may first use provider reports, available policies and written questionnaires.

12.3 The audit process, evidence types, frequency and costs must be completed in Annex 6. No independent certification or audit report is promised by this draft.

## 13. Liability, precedence and term

13.1 Liability, indemnities, monetary caps, exclusions and insurance must be agreed in the final Terms or an executed commercial schedule after legal review. This draft creates no unapproved cap or indemnity.

13.2 If this DPA conflicts with the Terms concerning processing of Customer Personal Data, this DPA will govern that specific processing subject matter to the extent legally enforceable. The Terms otherwise remain in effect.

13.3 This DPA takes effect only when its final version has been validly accepted by an authorised Institute Owner or separately executed by the parties. The acceptance method must create evidence of the document version, content hash, authenticated actor, Institute ID, timestamp and acceptance scope.
